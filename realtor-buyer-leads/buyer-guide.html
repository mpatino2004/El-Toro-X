The First-Time Home Buyer's Roadmap

Your step-by-step guide to buying with confidence

Before you start: Buying a home is a marathon, not a sprint. This guide walks you through each phase so you know what's coming — and what decisions matter most.

Phase 1: Get financially ready

Know your real numbers

Your max purchase price isn't just what a lender approves — it's what fits your monthly budget comfortably. Factor in property taxes, insurance, HOA fees, maintenance (budget 1% of home value per year), and utilities.

Check your credit

Most lenders want a score of 620+ for conventional loans; FHA may accept lower. Pull your free credit report, dispute errors, and avoid new credit cards or large purchases before applying.

Save for down payment AND closing costs

  • Down payment: 3–20% depending on loan type
  • Closing costs: Typically 2–5% of purchase price
  • Reserves: Lenders often want 2–6 months of payments in the bank after closing

Phase 2: Get pre-approved

Pre-approval is a lender's written commitment based on verified income, assets, and credit. It's stronger than a pre-qualification (which is just an estimate).

  • Gather pay stubs, W-2s, tax returns, bank statements
  • Compare at least 2–3 lenders for rates and fees
  • Ask about first-time buyer programs (FHA, VA, USDA, state/local grants)
  • Get a pre-approval letter before making offers

Phase 3: Define what you want

Make two lists: must-haves (bedrooms, commute, school district) and nice-to-haves (granite counters, pool). Be honest about trade-offs — perfect homes are rare in every price range.

Phase 4: Search and tour strategically

Don't tour homes above your pre-approved amount. Visit at different times of day. Check cell signal, noise, parking, and neighborhood feel. Take photos and notes — they blur together fast.

Red flags on tours: Water stains, cracks in foundation, musty smells, DIY electrical/plumbing, neighbors with neglected properties, busy roads you didn't notice on the map.

Phase 5: Make a competitive offer

Your agent will pull comparable sales ("comps") to price your offer. In competitive markets, consider:

  • Strong earnest money deposit
  • Flexible closing date for the seller
  • Minimal contingencies (but never waive inspection without understanding the risk)
  • Escalation clause or appraisal gap coverage if appropriate

Phase 6: Under contract — what happens next

  1. Earnest money deposited — shows you're serious; may be refundable during contingency periods
  2. Home inspection — hire your own inspector; negotiate repairs or credits
  3. Appraisal — lender verifies the home is worth the loan amount
  4. Final loan approval — don't change jobs or make large purchases
  5. Final walkthrough — confirm condition before closing
  6. Closing day — sign documents, get keys

First-time buyer programs to ask about

  • FHA loans — 3.5% down, more flexible credit
  • VA loans — 0% down for eligible veterans
  • USDA loans — 0% down in eligible rural/suburban areas
  • State & local grants — down payment assistance varies by location
  • Employer programs — some companies offer homebuyer benefits

Your pre-closing checklist

  • Transfer utilities to your name
  • Set up homeowner's insurance (required before closing)
  • Review Closing Disclosure 3 days before closing
  • Bring government ID and certified funds for closing costs
  • Change locks or rekey after closing
